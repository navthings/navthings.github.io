teaching lilbase to talk
2026-09-26

a base model just continues whatever you give it. ask it a question and it's as likely to write five more questions as to answer. turning it into a chat model means finetuning it on conversations so it learns the pattern: someone says something, the assistant answers, then stops.

i did this one overnight on my macbook air, in mlx. the data is smol-smoltalk, and the model only gets graded on the assistant's replies, not the user's messages. it ran 1,136 steps of 32k tokens, about 37m tokens. that's only around 12% of the dataset, but it's what the laptop could do in a night.

on conversations it had never seen, loss went from 2.230 for plain lilbase to 1.337.

what works

say "hi" and it answers "Hello! How can I help you today?" and stops. that sounds boring, but plain lilbase answers the same prompt by repeating the chat formatting forever. it does numbered lists and code blocks, and it wrote a correct one-line function to reverse a string (then explained it completely wrong).

what doesn't

it doesn't know things. it told me the capital of australia is melbourne, and on another try, perth. 17 + 25 came out as 48, followed by "so, the answer is 17". i asked for a haiku about a cat who hates mondays and got twenty lines ending in "a reminder that we are all brothers", over and over.

that isn't a bug in the finetune. finetuning teaches the format, not facts, and a 297m model that read 6 billion tokens just doesn't know much. turning the randomness (temperature) down from 0.7 to 0.4 helps though: 12 out of 12 normal replies to "hi" instead of 8 out of 12.
