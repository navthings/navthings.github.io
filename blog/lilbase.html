lilbase: a 297m model on a free kaggle tpu
2026-09-23

everything before this trained on my laptop. for lilbase i wanted a proper base model, one that has read a real chunk of the internet, and a macbook air was never going to get there. kaggle gives out free time on a tpu v5e-8, which is 8 of google's ai chips, so i moved there and rewrote the training in jax.

it's 297m parameters, llama-style: 24 layers, 1024 wide, 16 query heads sharing 4 key-value heads, with the llama tokenizer. it read 6.1 billion tokens of fineweb-edu, which is about right for its size by the chinchilla rule of roughly 20 tokens per parameter. each step is 524,288 tokens split across the 8 chips, and one kaggle session gets about 8.4 hours before it has to save and stop.

it beats gpt-2 small on two of three and loses on lambada. to be fair to gpt-2, it's from 2019, it read different data, and lilbase is more than twice its size. it's a fun comparison, not a fair fight.

things that went wrong: kaggle sometimes hands out a broken tpu with 1 working chip instead of 8, so the script now refuses to start unless it sees all 8. and doubling the batch ran out of chip memory by about 400mb.

ollama run navthings/lilbase "The water cycle begins when"

it's a base model, so it continues text rather than answering questions. the grammar is solid and the facts are confidently made up.
